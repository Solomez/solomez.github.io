A dating app built around events: I tried a ready-made design system, and it did not fit

In short

The service is for meeting new people, and not only for romance. Most dating apps today are built around looks; here you can meet a group of people at an event, have a good time together, and find friends or a relationship.

So far there are 18 concept screens and a sign-in flow of 24 screens. I also rebuilt two screens on the design system of that ID service; after calls with the developers, we design outside that system for now.

01 · The question: build on the ID service’s design system or not

The client proposed building the app on the ID service’s design system, with our own colors, fonts and spacing on top. The reason was speed of development.

By then I already had a visual concept of 18 screens.

02 · I built part of the concept on that system

I rebuilt two concept screens, the home page and the public profile, with the ID service’s ready-made components. After that we had a series of calls with the development team.

On the calls it turned out that we could not adapt the system: we could only assemble screens from ready-made parts, with no settings of our own, and that would have broken the concept.

For now we design the screens outside that system. The client has yet to decide which design system the app will use.

03 · From a list of events to one card per screen

In the first versions, events came as a list. Now there is one event on the screen, and the photo fills the screen behind the text.

I wanted to add a swipe, as in dating apps: people already know this mechanic. The concept went through three rounds of review, and most comments were about the swipe and the profile cards. Where the client and I saw things differently, we tried several versions; nothing was rejected.

04 · The profile is a photo and a feed from events

A profile opens with a photo. Below it is a feed of photos and videos from events. I designed four interactions for this feed: the header collapses on scroll, a double tap adds a like, a long press opens a preview of the event, and a photo opens full screen.

05 · Sign-in: 24 screens from the client’s requirements

The client gave us written requirements for the first sign-in, with the edge cases: an age limit, an error on the ID service’s side, a required update. From them I designed a flow of 24 screens, with a PIN code, Face ID and permission requests.

How I used AI

Claude helped me draw simple layouts that describe the logic of a few non-standard flows, such as swiping through events the way dating apps let you swipe through people. I designed the final screens and the other flows myself. Claude also checked my flows for errors, collected examples from the web into summaries, suggested some improvements, and helped with texts.

06 · I now plan five or six rounds of improvement from the start

So far the concept has had three review rounds. The next step is the client’s: he reviews the screens and decides on the design system.
