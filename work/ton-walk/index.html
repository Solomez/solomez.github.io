Six versions of one screen: a walk-to-earn game inside Telegram

Startup MVP, released · 2,000+ players at launch and a survey of 63 people

  • The only product designer
  • Studio
  • 2025
Context
TON Walk, a game inside Telegram where a player walks and earns TON, a cryptocurrency. An MVP for a startup, made at the studio O.dev (Ошка)
Role
the only product designer: interface, design system, flows, and interface texts in English
Team
product owner, QA, frontend, backend. I worked every day with the product managers on the TON Walk side, with some of them in English
When
first half of 2025, three months
Status
MVP released
AI
used only to edit the texts
Numbers from
Figma file, October 2026; the survey and test numbers are my counts from 2025

In short

The walk screen of this three-month MVP went through six versions, because the early ones did not show how to start a walk and what you get for it. In total I designed 94 screens in 9 sections and a design system of 46 component sets. In a prototype test with Google Forms, about nine in ten people reached the end of the scenario. The MVP was released

At launch and a little later the game had 2,000+ players. Later the startup closed because of the client's financial problems

01 · Most people did not understand how such apps work

I started with a closed survey in Google Forms. 63 people answered: 32 users of crypto wallets, 18 users of similar apps, and 13 people new to crypto. Asked what keeps them from starting, 62% chose the answer that they do not understand how it works. 79% chose a game-like presentation with visual hints, and 6% chose a technical description

02 · The walk screen went through six versions

A player walks in a chosen pair of sneakers, and the more they walk, the less energy they have. The first version had a map and one button

I found no good references for this screen, and in the early versions nothing on it gave a reason to go. The changes came from two sources: reviews with the product managers and logic errors that I found myself. What changed along the way:

  1. The walk became a separate screen without the bottom menu

  2. The yield is now counted in TON per kilometer, not TON per hour

  3. Energy turned into four segments with a timer

  4. The main number in the dial is now TON, not kilometers

  5. The speed moved next to that number, and the arc got colored zones

03 · A prototype test in Google Forms with three groups

I tested the design with three groups of 10 to 12 people to find UX problems. The test ran in closed Google Forms, and some of the forms came with a link to the clickable prototype. By my count at the time, about nine in ten reached the end of the scenario, and almost as many understood how earning works

In the surveys people asked for more game elements, such as bonuses and achievements. After that the visual style changed with each iteration

04 · A walk can stop in three ways, and each has its own screen

I drew these states before the build. If a player goes faster than 15 km/h, that part of the walk is not counted, and the finish screen shows the amount that was not counted

I split the flows by the state of the player: a first entry with an empty balance, a player with a balance, and a player with referrals. For development I laid out 78 more screens on seven pages, from sign-up to the walk

05 · The wallet stayed in TON: we cut two features to ship the MVP

I also designed the in-game wallet. A player tops up by scanning a QR code or copying the address. Before a withdrawal the screen shows the fee, the minimum amount and how many withdrawals are left today, with a limit of one a day

We removed Telegram Stars, a second in-app currency for top-ups and withdrawals, to speed up development. Loot boxes in the shop and the inventory also left the MVP, with a plan to return to them later

06 · The design system: 46 component sets and colors named by role

The system has 46 component sets and 384 variants. The 145 color tokens are named by role, for example component/accent/primary/hover, and each one points to a color from the palette. The file has 256 variables in 5 collections, all in one dark mode, and 20 text styles in Onest. I drew states in advance: the inventory card has a drag-and-drop state, and input fields have six states

The TON Walk team made the name and the logo

07 · What I would do differently: show users several versions of the key screens

Today I would build a web version of the app and survey potential users with several versions of the key screens: it is hard to design one version that suits everyone perfectly

I would also fix the contrast of button labels. White text on the background is fine: 19.7. White labels on filled buttons do not pass: 3.74 on the accent button, 3.14 on the blue one, and 2.62 on the orange one. The WCAG AA minimum for text of this size is 4.5. A darker fill or a dark label would fix it
