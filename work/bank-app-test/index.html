A sign-in flow for a business banking app: three rounds of hypotheses, then a survey

Context
a test assignment from a bank: rethink the first sign-in to its iOS app for business
Role
product designer, working alone
When

Status

AI

The bank is not named, its logo is removed from the screens, and the other banks are shown as Bank A, B and C

In short

The task: the old sign-in works but looks dry. I reviewed other banks, tested hypotheses in three rounds, talked to a fintech designer and ran a survey

The main decision in my final flow: if a person enters a phone number the bank does not know, they go straight to opening an account, so a new client is not lost at sign-in

01 · How other banks start

I went through three other banks' apps from launch to sign-in. Banners and brand videos set the mood, but in one app the sign-in button gets lost against the video

02 · 12 hypotheses in three rounds

In each round I drew the screen, wrote hypotheses and checked each one against articles, competitors' apps or designers, then fixed the screen

The first round had a phone-only sign-in. My notes showed that business banking needs more for security, so login and password came back in the second round

03 · A fintech designer and 21 people

Then I showed the screens to a product designer with over five years in fintech. After our talk I removed the theme switch and improved the contrast

Next came a survey in Google Forms among people I know and members of chats and forums on the topic: 21 people rated the visual design, and 14 wrote what they liked and what they did not. I sorted the feedback by part of the flow

04 · The final flow and why it works this way

The final flow: splash, phone or login, SMS code, password, code word, PIN and Face ID

Unknown numbers lead to opening an account, as on the bank's website. If the code does not come, a button leads to support or ready answers. After the feedback I dropped the animation at the bottom of the screen and kept the brush animation on the splash, which most people liked

I also made a small UI kit and color tokens for light and dark themes

Writing hypotheses alone was the hardest part: usually I do it with a product owner or a second designer. Next I would run one or two more surveys with the bank's clients
