In short

I designed the home page with finance widgets, the deals section with a 13-column table and a Gantt view, and two sign-in flows, all from a written spec

The layout and the drag and drop features were my decisions. I talked with the lead designer all the time, and the lead's edits were small UX improvements

01 · The home page: balances, currencies and exchange rates

The home page opens with news and a grid of 16 widget cards, 10 designed and 6 still placeholders. The money widgets show turnover by region, turnover and payments in six currencies, the total balance and the company wallets. Exchange rate differences come as a chart, switching between volume and rate, or as a sortable table

02 · A deals table with 13 columns

All deals sit in one table with 13 columns, from ID, type and amount to status, deadline, counterparty and assignee. Rows come in two groups, current and queued. Filters open from 8 column headers and sorting from 9. Two tabs stay pinned, all deals and deals that need action, and an employee adds their own

Rows selected in both groups get one comment or one tag at once, and a missing tag is created from its search. Export asks for the tabs and the file format

03 · The same deals on a Gantt chart

A switch next to the search turns the list into a Gantt chart. Each deal is a bar on the timeline and opens into its actions. A red line marks today, and weekends are shaded

04 · Two sign-in flows and the design system

I designed sign-in for two roles. On one role's map, 9 screens follow 5 branches: first sign-in with a new password, repeat sign-in by email or phone, an authenticator code, a deactivated account and a four-step password reset. A note on the map records one agreement: the phone field gets a country picker

I built these screens in the platform's existing design system and extended it. That file has not survived
