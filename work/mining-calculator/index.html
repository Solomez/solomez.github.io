A mining calculator: a six-column table that also works on a phone

Online store for mining hardware, the calculator is live · buyers see payback and profit for each miner

  • The only designer
  • Private client
  • 2025-2026
Context
Miningx100, an online store for mining hardware
Role
the only designer on the project, for a private client. The calculator's UX and UI are mine, from the first version to the review of the developers' build. The formulas are not mine
When
2025-2026
Status
launched
Site
miningx100.ru/calculator
AI
used for the interface copy

The client asked for the calculator after we looked at competitors. In this market a calculator is a common tool. A buyer enters the electricity price and the pool fee and sees the payback period, income, costs and profit for each miner. There are two tabs: By model lists every miner on sale, and Custom calculation lets a buyer enter the settings of their own miner

01 · Six sortable columns, then cards on a phone

On wide screens the calculator is one table with six sortable columns: model, price, payback, income, costs and profit. From 768 px down each miner becomes a card with the model and four numbers. The six column sorts became one sort menu above the cards, and pages became a "Load 6 more" button

02 · The buyer's journey in seven stages

I also mapped the buyer's journey in seven stages, from the first search to a repeat order. At the decision and purchase stages the map lists unclear payback and a long order form among the pains. On the finished site, the calculator shows payback for each model, and a request takes two fields, name and phone

03 · Miners on the first phone screen

In the first phone version all the settings came before the list, so a buyer saw no miners on the first screen. In the final version I moved the algorithm, manufacturer and cooling filters under a "Filter" button so that the list of miners shows at once; only the electricity price and the pool fee stay on screen. The first card moved up from 1,236 px to 816 px, into the first phone screen, and each card got 38% shorter

04 · Filters, an empty result and 11 states

On a computer the filters apply at once, and an empty result keeps the chosen filters with "Reset all"; on a phone they open in a panel with an "Apply" button. For developers I specified 11 states for four screen sizes: a sticky table header, the end of the list, hints on each coin and setting, an empty result. The same payback calculation sits on the product page next to the price and "Quick order", so a buyer sees it before buying

05 · What I learned

On a phone, settings compete with results, so only what a buyer changes on every visit stays in view and the rest goes under one button. And when a table turns into cards, the column headers disappear, so sorting needs a control of its own
