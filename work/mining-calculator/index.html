A mining calculator: a six-column table that also works on a phone

  • The only designer
  • Private client
  • 2025–2026
Context
Miningx100, an online store for mining hardware
Role
the only designer on the project, for a private client; the calculator is mine, UX and UI
When
2025–2026
Status
launched
Site
miningx100.ru/calculator
AI
used for the interface copy

The client asked for the calculator after we looked at competitors. A buyer enters the electricity price and the pool fee and sees the payback period, income, costs and profit for each miner. On wide screens it is one table with six sortable columns: model, price, payback, income, costs and profit. From 768 px down each miner becomes a card with the model and four numbers; the six column sorts became one “Most profitable” menu, and pages became a “Load 6 more” button.

In the first phone version all the settings came before the list, so a buyer saw no miners on the first screen. In the final version the algorithm, manufacturer and cooling filters sit under a “Filter” button, and only the electricity price and the pool fee stay on screen. The first card moved up from 1,236 px to 816 px, into the first phone screen, and each card got 38% shorter.

On a computer the filters apply at once, and an empty result keeps the chosen filters with “Reset all”; on a phone they open in a panel with an “Apply” button. For developers I specified 11 states for four screen sizes: a sticky table header, the end of the list, hints on each coin and setting, an empty result. The same payback calculation sits on the product page next to the price and “Quick order”, so a buyer sees it before buying. The site is in Russian; the screens here are translated.

What I learned: on a phone, settings compete with results, so only what a buyer changes on every visit stays in view and the rest goes under one button. And when a table turns into cards, the column headers disappear, so sorting needs a control of its own. Next time I would also plan more time for the design work and set out the tasks and their deadlines before starting.
